Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is a court marriage?

A court marriage is a legal union solemnized by a Marriage Officer under the Special Marriage Act, 1954. It is conducted directly through the government without any religious rituals or ceremonies, and it is open to eligible Indian citizens regardless of their religion, caste, or creed.

2. What are the basic eligibility requirements for a court marriage?

To be eligible for a court marriage in India, the parties must fulfill the following conditions at the time of marriage:

  • Age: The bride must be at least 18 years old, and the groom must be at least 21 years old.
  • Mental Capacity: Both parties must be capable of giving valid consent and must meet the legal conditions relating to mental capacity.
  • Monogamy: Neither party should have a living spouse at the time of marriage.
  • Prohibited Degrees: The parties must not fall within the degrees of prohibited relationships specified by law, unless an applicable custom permits it.

3. What documents are required for a court marriage?

Generally, both parties need to submit the following documents along with the application form:

  • Completed application form with the prescribed fee.
  • Passport-size photographs of both parties.
  • Proof of Age: Birth certificate, Matriculation/School leaving certificate, or Passport.
  • Proof of Address/Residence: Aadhaar card, Voter ID, Passport, or Utility bill.
  • Affidavit: Stating marital status, date of birth, and other required declarations.
  • If previously married: Divorce decree or death certificate of the former spouse, as applicable.
  • Witness Documents: Identity proof, address proof, and photographs of three witnesses.

4. What is the procedure for a court marriage?

  • Notice of Intended Marriage: The couple must submit a notice to the Marriage Officer of the district where at least one party has resided for not less than 30 days immediately before the notice.
  • Publication of Notice: The Marriage Officer publishes the notice at the office as required by law.
  • Waiting Period (30 Days): Legal objections may be raised within 30 days from publication.
  • Solemnization: If there are no valid objections, the couple and three witnesses appear before the Marriage Officer to complete the legal formalities.

5. Who can act as a witness for a court marriage?

Any responsible person who can properly identify the bride and groom can act as a witness. Typically, three witnesses are required. They can be friends, relatives, or colleagues, and they must carry valid identity and address proof.

6. How long does the entire court marriage process take?

Under the Special Marriage Act, a 30-day notice period generally applies. In practice, depending on the district marriage office's schedule and paperwork verification, the total process may take longer.
